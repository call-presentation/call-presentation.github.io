Frequently Asked Questions

About the Twelve Imams

Who are the Twelve Imams?

They are twelve divinely appointed leaders from the Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ family, beginning with Imam Ali (ع) and ending with Imam al-Mahdi (عج). Each Imam was designated by divine command and inherited knowledge and authority from the Prophet.

Why is Imam al-Mahdi called "The Seal of the Imams"?

Just as Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) is the Seal of the Prophets, Imam al-Mahdi (عج) is the Seal of the Imams — the final divinely appointed Imam, completing the succession that began with Imam Ali (ع).

Are the Twelve Imams mentioned in both Sunni and Shia sources?

Yes. The hadith about twelve successors appears in major Sunni collections including Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. Shia sources additionally identify these twelve as the Imams from the Ahl al-Bayt, starting with Imam Ali and concluding with Imam al-Mahdi.

What is the significance of these twelve leaders?

The Twelve Imams are spiritual and worldly guides for the Muslim community. They preserve and explain divine knowledge, interpret the Qur'an and Sunnah with divinely granted understanding, and maintain prophetic guidance after the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) until the Day of Judgment.

What is Imam al-Mahdi's role today?

In Shia belief, Imam al-Mahdi (عج) is alive but in occultation (hidden from public view). He will reappear by divine command to establish justice and fulfill the promise of a just world order. Until then, he remains the living Imam, guiding believers and awaiting the appointed time.
