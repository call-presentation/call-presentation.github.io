The Báb: Examining His Claims

A critical overview of the Báb's life, evolving claims, and theological inconsistencies — examining whether his assertions align with Islamic criteria for Imam al-Mahdi (عج).

Introduction Background Who Is the Mahdi? Key Issues Contradictions FAQ References

Introduction

The Báb was a 19th-century figure from Shiraz, Iran, who initially claimed to be a spiritual "gate" (Báb) to Imam al-Mahdi (عج). Over time, his claims changed and evolved, eventually leading many people to believe that he was asserting a divinely appointed role beyond what Shia Islam allows.

Who Was the Báb?

  • Name: Sayyid Ali Muhammad Shirazi (1819–1850)
  • Origin: Shiraz, Iran
  • Initial claim: The "Báb" (gate) to the Hidden Imam
  • Later development: Escalation and change of claims

Who Is the Mahdi?

In Shia Islam, Imam al-Mahdi (عج) holds a divinely appointed role. Throughout history, some individuals have claimed to be the Mahdi, but they fail to meet the required criteria.

A divine position granted by Allah (swt) requires consistency, divine knowledge, and the fulfillment of the mission of justice. According to this analysis, the Báb did not meet these requirements.

Key Issues With the Báb's Claims

The Báb's Repentance

When the Báb claimed to be the Qāʾim, many realized he was asserting something he could not uphold. He was brought before Iranian authorities, including judges and the crown prince Nasser al-Din Shah Qajar. During this examination, he repented and begged for mercy.

This repentance is documented within Baha'i sources themselves. In the book Kashf al-Ghitaʾ ʿan Hiyal al-Aʿdaʾ by Abolfazl Golpaygani, a Baha'i scholar, the repentance of the Báb is discussed.

If his claim had true divine legitimacy, he would not have retracted it under pressure. This demonstrates a major inconsistency.

Lack of Divine Knowledge

The infallible Imams possess divine knowledge and do not require human teachers. However, Babi and Baha'i sources state that the Báb attended a maktab to learn Arabic and literature.

Requiring formal education from human teachers contradicts the characteristics of Imam al-Mahdi (عج).

Lack of Justice and Peace

Imam Mahdi's (عج) mission is to establish global peace and justice. During the Báb's time, this did not occur. Instead, Babi followers initiated three wars within Qajar Iran, resulting in the deaths of many innocent people.

In the Báb's first book, he reportedly instructed followers to kill those who refused to accept his message and to destroy non-Babi religious texts and sites. This approach directly contradicts the Mahdi's role of guiding people with justice and mercy.

Escalation and Contradictions

One of the strongest arguments against the Báb is the inconsistency of his claims. The Imams (as) were clear, consistent, and divinely designated. The Báb's evolving claims conflict with Islamic teachings and even with Babi and Baha'i writings.

According to Babi and Baha'i belief, the Báb changed his claim six times:

  1. Claimed "dikriyat" as sayyid-e-dhikr
  2. Claimed "babiyat" (the Gate)
  3. Claimed Mahdihood
  4. Claimed prophethood
  5. Claimed lordship
  6. Claimed divinity

These contradictions undermine the credibility of his claims and oppose the finality of Islamic law and the authority of the Twelve Imams.
