The Báb: Examining His Claims

A critical look at the Báb's life, changing claims, and theological inconsistencies — and whether they meet Islamic criteria for Imam al-Mahdi (عج).

Introduction

The Báb was a 19th-century figure from Shiraz, Iran, who first claimed to be a spiritual "gate" (Báb) to Imam al-Mahdi (عج). Over time, his claims evolved, leading many to believe he was asserting a divine role beyond what Shia Islam allows.

Who Was the Báb?

  • Name: Sayyid Ali Muhammad Shirazi (1819–1850)
  • Origin: Shiraz, Iran
  • Initial claim: The "Báb" (gate) to the Hidden Imam
  • Later development: Escalation and change of claims

Who Is the Mahdi?

In Shia Islam, Imam al-Mahdi (عج) holds a divinely appointed role. Throughout history, some individuals have claimed to be the Mahdi, but they fail to meet the required criteria.

A divine position from Allah (swt) requires consistency, divine knowledge, and fulfilling the mission of justice. By this standard, the Báb did not qualify.

Key Issues With the Báb's Claims

The Báb's Repentance

When the Báb claimed to be the Qāʾim, many saw he could not support this claim. He was brought before Iranian authorities, including judges and Crown Prince Nasser al-Din Mirza Qajar, where he repented and begged for mercy.

This repentance is documented in Baha'i sources themselves. Abolfazl Golpaygani, a Baha'i scholar, discusses it in Kashf al-Ghitāʾ ʿan Ḥiyal al-Aʿdāʾ.

If his claim were truly divine, he would not have retracted it under pressure — a major inconsistency.

Lack of Divine Knowledge

The infallible Imams possess divine knowledge and do not require human teachers. However, Babi and Baha'i sources state that the Báb attended a maktab to learn Arabic and literature.

Needing formal education contradicts the qualities expected of Imam al-Mahdi (عج).

Lack of Justice and Peace

Imam Mahdi's (عج) mission is to establish global peace and justice. During the Báb's time, this did not occur. Instead, Babi followers initiated three wars within Qajar Iran, resulting in the deaths of many innocent people.

In his first book, the Báb reportedly told followers to kill those who rejected his message and destroy non-Babi texts and sites. This directly contradicts the Mahdi's role of guiding people with justice and mercy.

Escalation and Contradictions

One of the strongest arguments against the Báb is how inconsistent his claims were. The Imams (as) were clear, consistent, and divinely appointed. The Báb's changing claims conflict with Islamic teachings and even Babi and Baha'i writings.

According to Babi and Baha'i belief, the Báb changed his claim six times:

  1. Claimed "dikriyat" as sayyid-e-dhikr
  2. Claimed "babiyat" (the Gate)
  3. Claimed Ghamiyat
  4. Claimed prophethood
  5. Claimed lordship
  6. Claimed divinity

These contradictions undermine the credibility of his claims and oppose the finality of Islamic law and the authority of the Twelve Imams.
