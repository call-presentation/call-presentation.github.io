Would Allah ever leave humanity without guidance? The Qur’an clearly tells us that he would not. (13:7) In the Sermon of Ghadir, the Prophet addresses our guidance with a specific declaration about the final Imam. He says: "Ala innahu al-baqi hujja wa la hujja ba'dahu." “Indeed, he is the remaining Proof—and there is no proof after him.” [1] To understand this we should first understand the concept of a divine proof, or a hujjah. Imam al-Ṣādiq teaches us a universal rule: “The earth can never remain without a Hujjah from Allah.” [2] But why is that hujjah needed?? The Qur’an explains in Surah An-Nisa that divine guides are sent "so that mankind will have no argument against Allah after the messengers." [3] This creates a theological necessity for a point of contact between the Divine and mankind to remove any excuses for ignorance. However, the Prophet’s declaration adds a critical constraint: Finality. By stating he is the "Remaining Proof," the Prophet is defining the Mahdi as the culmination of that divine argument. He is the point where the necessity of guidance meets the completion of its purpose. So, I ask you: If Imam Mahdi is the final proof, what does that mean for anyone claiming divine leadership after him? It implies that any such claim is structurally impossible. This declaration closes the door on future innovation in divine authority. It establishes that the roadmap is complete; the argument is finished, and no further guides are required to supersede him.
