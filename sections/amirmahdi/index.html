Introduction

The authority of Imam Al-Mahdi (عج) is not a matter of human selection or political process. It is rooted in divine designation—a deliberate choice made by Allah Himself. This presentation explores the Quranic foundations for this claim and demonstrates why divine appointment, not human preference, determines true leadership.

The Hadith: Allah's Chosen One

اَلا اِنَّهُ خِیَرَةُ اللَّهِ وَ مُخْتارُهُ
"Indeed, he is Allah's chosen and selected one."

This hadith establishes that Imam Al-Mahdi was chosen specifically by Allah. His authority does not emerge from human consensus, political maneuvering, or popular support. It flows from divine designation.

But what evidence supports this claim? The answer lies in the Qur'an itself, which establishes the nature of divinely appointed guidance.

The Eternal Guide on Earth

The Qur'an establishes a fundamental principle: the earth is never without a guide appointed by Allah. This is not a temporary arrangement, but a permanent feature of divine mercy.

The Quran on Guidance

إِنَّمَآ أَنتَ مُنذِرٌۭ وَلِكُلِّ قَوْمٍ هَادٍ
"(O Prophet), you are only a warner, and for every people there is a guide."
Surah Ar-Ra'ad, verse 7

Who is this guide? Imam As-Sadiq (ع) clarifies:

"Every Imam is a guide for the generation in which he is."
— Kitab Al-Kafi, vol. 1, p. 191

The guide refers to the Imams. This guidance is not limited to the lifetime of the Prophet (ص). It extends to every generation.

The Continuous Succession

وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى جَاعِلٌۭ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةًۭ
"And when your Lord said to the angels, 'I am going to place a successive vicegerent on earth.'"
Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 30

The grammatical structure of the Arabic implies continuity—that the earth is never empty of God's successor. Al-Alusi, a Sunni scholar, explains in his exegesis Ruh al-Ma'ani:

"[That] this caliphate will remain in the perfect human being until the Day of Judgment and the Hour of Resurrection."
The Imams are a guide for every generation, and the guide exists for every era until the Day of Judgement.

Those in Authority (Ulu al-Amr)

The Qur'an goes further—it commands obedience to these divinely appointed guides with the same weight as obedience to Allah and His Messenger.

يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ أَطِيعُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا۟ ٱلرَّسُولَ وَأُو۟لِى ٱلْأَمْرِ مِنكُمْ
"O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you."
Surah An-Nisa, verse 59

Obedience to Allah is absolute. Obedience to the Messenger is absolute. And in this verse, obedience to "those in authority" (ulu al-amr) is given the same weight.

Who Are the Ulu al-Amr?

Imam As-Sadiq (ع) clarifies:

"The Imams are the leaders of authority from the descendants of Ali and Fatima, peace be upon them, until the Hour comes."
— Kamal Al-Din

The ulu al-amr specifically refer to the Imams. And the final leader of authority is Imam Al-Mahdi (عج)—the final infallible descendant of Imam Ali and Lady Fatima.

Why Divine Appointment Requires Infallibility

The command for absolute obedience carries a logical implication: the authoritative figures must be infallible.

Allah would not command unconditional obedience to someone prone to error. To do so would be to potentially command us to follow error itself—a contradiction of divine wisdom.

Guidance cannot be appointed by human choice or democracy, as these methods are inherently subject to fallibility.

Only divine appointment ensures that the guide is free from error, capable of leading humanity without deviation from the straight path.

Conclusion

The Qur'an establishes a clear framework for understanding Imamate:

  • The earth is never without a divinely appointed guide
  • That guide is chosen by Allah, not by human selection
  • The Qur'an commands absolute obedience to these guides
  • The guides are the Imams from the descendants of Imam Ali and Lady Fatima
  • Their authority continues until the Hour
  • Imam al-Mahdi is the final guide until the End of Times

Therefore, divine leadership is appointed exclusively by Allah. No individual can legitimately claim such authority through self-appointment or popular support.

Imam Al-Mahdi (عج) is Allah's chosen one—not by human consensus, but by divine decree.
